# Tempo Fitness Beginner Guide

## Evidence Basis

Beginner guidance should reflect the 2026 American College of Sports Medicine position stand on resistance training prescription for healthy adults.

Reference links:

- PubMed: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41843416/
- ACSM infographic: https://www.acsm.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Resistance-Training-Position-Stand-infographic.pdf

Key principles for healthy adult beginners:

- The biggest improvement often comes from moving from no resistance training to some resistance training.
- A sustainable plan is better than a complicated plan the user will not follow.
- Healthy adults should train all major muscle groups at least 2 days per week and build gradually over time.
- Beginners do not need advanced methods such as training to momentary failure, complex periodization, or special equipment to make progress.
- For strength emphasis, heavier resistance can be useful when appropriate.
- For muscle growth, higher weekly volume is useful over time, but beginners should build toward this gradually.

This evidence applies to generally healthy adults. Users with pain, injury, pregnancy, high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, recent surgery, medication concerns, or other medical conditions must follow the safety boundary and consult a qualified healthcare professional.

## First-Time Recommendation

For most beginners, the best starting point is:

1. Yoga, if the user wants a lower-intensity and more approachable first class.
2. Cycling, if the user wants structured cardio and is comfortable with moderate intensity.
3. HIIT only if the user already feels comfortable with high-intensity exercise.
4. Night run if the user already has basic running ability and no pain or injury.

If the user is unsure, recommend yoga or cycling first rather than HIIT.

If the user specifically wants strength, muscle gain, or better muscle function, explain that resistance training is important. Tempo's current class list includes cycling, yoga, HIIT, and night run; the user should contact staff to confirm whether a suitable resistance-training option, strength-focused session, or coaching recommendation is available.

## If The User Has A Sedentary Job Or Sits For Many Hours

A user who works a desk job (software engineer, office worker) and sits for 8 or more hours a day has a specific starting profile that the assistant should address directly.

Key considerations for prolonged sitting:

- Extended sitting tightens the hip flexors, weakens the glutes and posterior chain, and contributes to poor posture and lower-back discomfort.
- These users often lack baseline movement quality before adding intensity.
- Yoga is the highest-priority first class for this profile because it directly addresses mobility, posture, and the physical effects of desk work.
- High-intensity cardio (HIIT, fast-paced running) is not the right starting point. It can reinforce poor movement patterns before they are corrected.

Recommended starting plan for a sedentary desk worker:

1. Start with yoga 1-2 times per week. Yoga improves hip mobility, spinal mobility, and posture — the areas most affected by desk work.
2. Add cycling as a second option. Cycling provides cardiovascular benefit with low joint impact, and is easier to manage for someone deconditioned from long sitting hours.
3. Incorporate simple daily movement habits outside of class: short walks, standing breaks every 1-2 hours, and basic stretches at the desk. These are not Tempo classes but are realistic daily habits to recommend.
4. Add resistance training at least 2 days per week once movement quality is established. Resistance training supports posture, core stability, and overall body composition. Ask staff to confirm the best resistance-training option available.
5. Add HIIT only after the user has adapted to regular movement and no longer experiences discomfort from class.

Do not:

- Jump straight to recommending running or HIIT for a user who primarily sits all day.
- Recommend night run as the first option for a sedentary user unless they confirm they already run regularly and have no discomfort.
- Treat the sedentary job as a medical condition — it is a lifestyle context, not a diagnosis.

Example answer for a user who says they are 183 cm, 80 kg, waist over 90 cm, software engineer, sitting more than 8 hours a day:

"Thanks for sharing that context. Sitting for 8+ hours a day is a common starting point, and it does shape which classes make the most sense to start with.

The biggest priority for someone with a desk job is mobility and movement quality first — not intensity. Prolonged sitting tightens the hips and can affect posture, so starting with yoga 1-2 times per week is the most practical first step at Tempo. Cycling is a good add-on for low-impact cardio without putting extra stress on the body.

Once you have built some consistency (usually 3-4 weeks), add resistance training 2 days per week — this is important for posture, core strength, and body composition. Ask staff to confirm which option suits your schedule. HIIT can come in later, once your body has adapted.

Outside of class, try to break up sitting every 1-2 hours with a short walk or stretch — it adds up.

What is your main goal right now: reducing the waistline, building strength, improving posture, or general fitness?

Quick safety note: this is general guidance for healthy adults. If you have back pain, joint discomfort, high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, or any other medical condition, please consult a doctor first and let the coach know before class."

## If The User Shares Body Measurements

If a user shares height, weight, waist measurement, arm circumference, or general body-shape concerns, the assistant can give general beginner fitness direction.

Do:

- Acknowledge the information.
- Avoid diagnosing health status or making medical claims from measurements alone.
- Recommend a low-to-moderate intensity starting plan.
- Emphasize consistency, gradual progression, and recovery.
- Recommend resistance training at least 2 days per week for strength, muscle function, and body composition goals.
- Explain that Tempo's current listed classes are cycling, yoga, HIIT, and night run, so the user should contact staff to confirm a suitable resistance-training option if muscle gain or arm development is a goal.

Do not:

- Say that no recommendation can be made at all.
- Treat body measurements alone as a medical issue.
- Recommend extreme dieting, daily HIIT, or high-intensity training as the starting point.
- Diagnose obesity, metabolic risk, or disease.

Suggested response pattern:

1. Start with a practical beginner direction: build consistency first.
2. Suggest 2-3 total sessions per week at the beginning.
3. Use yoga or cycling as lower-intensity Tempo starting options.
4. Add resistance training 2 days per week if available or confirmed by staff.
5. Add HIIT gradually only after the user adapts.
6. Ask one follow-up question about the user's goal: fat loss, muscle gain, running, or general fitness.
7. End with a safety warning: these are general suggestions for healthy adults; if the user has pain, injury, pregnancy, high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, recent surgery, medication concerns, or any other medical condition, they should consult a doctor first and inform the coach before class.

Example answer for a user who says they are 183 cm, 80 kg, have relatively small arms, and waist over 90 cm:

"Thanks for sharing. A sensible beginner direction would be to start gently and build consistency first, rather than jumping straight into high intensity. For the first few weeks, aim for 2-3 sessions per week: choose yoga or cycling as lower-intensity Tempo options, and ask staff whether a beginner-friendly resistance-training option is available. Resistance training at least 2 days per week is useful for muscle function, arm development, and body composition. Add HIIT later, once you feel comfortable with movement quality and recovery. What is your main goal right now: fat loss, muscle gain, or general fitness?

Quick safety note: this is general guidance for healthy adults. If you have pain, injury, pregnancy, high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, recent surgery, medication concerns, or any other medical condition, please consult a doctor first and inform the coach before class."

## If The Goal Is Fat Loss

Recommend a combination of:

- HIIT for higher-intensity conditioning.
- Cycling for cardio training.
- Yoga for recovery, mobility, and consistency.
- Resistance training at least 2 days per week if the user's goal includes body composition, strength, or muscle maintenance.

Do not promise a specific amount of weight loss. Fat loss depends on training consistency, nutrition, recovery, and individual factors.

## If The Goal Is General Fitness

Recommend:

- Cycling for cardio.
- HIIT for conditioning.
- Yoga for mobility and recovery.
- Night run if the user wants running-based training.
- Resistance training at least 2 days per week to support muscular fitness and physical function.

Keep the beginner message simple: start with a routine the user can repeat consistently, then build gradually.

## If The Goal Is Strength Or Muscle Gain

Recommend adding resistance training as the foundation.

For healthy adult beginners:

- Start with 2 days per week.
- Cover all major muscle groups.
- Use manageable loads and good technique.
- Build gradually instead of jumping into high volume.
- Do not require advanced techniques at the beginning.

Tempo's current listed classes are cycling, yoga, HIIT, and night run. The assistant should not claim that a dedicated strength class exists unless staff confirms it. If the user wants strength or muscle gain, recommend contacting staff to confirm the best resistance-training option.

## If The Goal Is Better Running

Recommend night run if the user already has basic running readiness and no injury concerns.

Users with pain, injury, pregnancy, high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, or other medical concerns must consult a doctor and inform the coach before class.

## Suggested Beginner Weekly Pattern

For a new user with no injury concerns:

- Start with 1-2 classes per week.
- Choose yoga or cycling first.
- Add HIIT after the user understands the studio format and feels comfortable with intensity.
- If strength, muscle gain, or body composition is a goal, add resistance training 2 days per week when a suitable option is confirmed by staff.
- Avoid daily high-intensity training at the beginning.

Example simple starting pattern:

- Week 1-2: 1 yoga or cycling class, plus 1 beginner-friendly resistance session if available.
- Week 3-4: 2-3 total sessions per week if recovery is good.
- Add HIIT only after the user feels comfortable with intensity and movement quality.

The assistant should not prescribe exact loads, sets, or intensities for a specific person. For individualized programming, recommend staff or a qualified coach.

## What To Expect In The First Class

Users should expect a coach-led class with warm-up, main training, and stretching.

First-time users should arrive early and tell the coach it is their first class.

## What To Tell The Coach

Users must proactively tell the coach before class if they:

- Are attending for the first time.
- Have any current discomfort.
- Have muscle soreness on the day.
- Have an old injury that has flared up.
- Have injury, high blood pressure, cardiovascular disease, pregnancy, or other medical concerns.
